Spain vs Argentina H2H and rivalry: record between both teams in the World Cup final match
Spain
and
Argentina
have never met in a
World Cup final
before, this Sunday will be their first. They have faced each other just once at the World Cup, a
2-1 Argentina win in the 1966 group stage
. It is also just the
second Spanish-speaking final in World Cup history
, following the 1930 match between
Uruguay and Argentina
.
Away from the
World Cup
, the rivalry is balanced but full of memorable games. In their last five non-World Cup encounters,
all of which were friendlies
, Spain won 6-1 in
2018
, Argentina won 4-1 in
2010
, Spain won 2-1 in
2009
, Spain won 2-1 in
2006
, and Argentina won 2-1 in
1999
.
With this game
Spain has reached the World Cup final twice
(2010 win). While
Argentina have been to six finals
(wins in 1978, 1986 and 2022; losses in 1930, 1990 and 2014). The rivalry has always carried extra passion. From classic friendlies to this historic final in New York, football fans around the world are in for a treat.
Spain vs Argentina prediction: Which team is more likely to win the 2026 World Cup?
For the first time in history, the
champions of Europe and South America
were slated to face off in the
"Finalissima,"
a match that could have served as a prelude to this summer's
World Cup final
. The encounter between the defending Euro and Copa América champions was originally scheduled for
March 27, 2026
, at Lusail Stadium in Qatar. But the escalating security situation in the
Middle East
forced FIFA to cancel the game.
Now, as if by fate, the two teams meet in the actual
World Cup final in New York
. Recent days have brought challenging conditions, heavy rain and poor air quality due to
Canadian wildfires
, likened to smoking the equivalent of 10 cigarettes a day. Whether the smoke clears by kick-off remains uncertain.
Bookmakers slightly favour
Spain
thanks to their disciplined style and rock-solid defence, they have conceded just
one goal in seven matches
. A second World Cup title would be historic, 16 years after 2010, and could see them set a new record for the
fewest goals conceded
by a champion.
Yet history in World Cup finals favours underdogs in certain ways. Since 2002, only
Argentina
has won the trophy as an underdog (Qatar 2022). All others entered as favourites. Argentina, defending their 2022 crown, seek to become the
third team to win back-to-back titles
, the first since Brazil in 1958-62. A fourth title would tie them with
Italy
and
Germany
for second place behind Brazil's five.
